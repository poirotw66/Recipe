intro: Pair the fruit sweetness of dried apricots with savory soy sauce in a
  single-serving pork rice dish. Loosen chilled cooked rice, cook and rest the
  pork separately, then stir-fry the aromatics, apricots, and rice before
  returning the pork. This version adapts a published ingredient pairing and
  fried-rice techniques; it has not been kitchen-tested by Bloom Kitchen.
steps:
  - "Pat the pork loin dry and cut it into similar slices about 1 cm thick. Dice the apricots, slice the scallion and separate its white and green parts, mince the garlic, and grate the ginger. Loosen any clumps in the chilled cooked rice with your fingers or a fork."
  - "Heat the oil in a skillet over medium heat. Cook the pork in one layer until lightly browned on both sides; insert the thermometer from the side into the thickest slice. Remove it at 63°C / 145°F and rest for at least 3 minutes. Continue cooking and recheck if needed. Wash any plate or tongs that touched raw pork before reusing them."
  - "In the same pan, cook the garlic, ginger, and scallion whites until fragrant, then add the apricots and stir-fry for about 30 seconds. Add the chilled rice, soy sauce, and rice vinegar. Toss and gently press apart clumps until the grains loosen and the rice is steaming throughout."
  - "Return the pork and any resting juices to the pan and toss until the whole dish is hot. Finish with scallion greens and black pepper."
tips:
  - Chilled cooked rice is drier and easier to separate in the pan. If using
    freshly cooked rice, spread it on a clean, shallow tray to cool quickly and
    refrigerate it within 1 hour. Keep the pork slices a similar thickness and
    check the thickest one for 63°C / 145°F, followed by a 3-minute rest. If
    using ground pork instead, cook it to 71°C / 160°F.
storage: Cool cooked rice quickly, refrigerate it within 1 hour, and use it
  within 24 hours. This recipe heats the rice through, so serve it freshly made.
  If any remains, divide it into a shallow container, cool it quickly,
  refrigerate it within 1 hour, eat it within 24 hours, and do not reheat it
  again.

Dried apricots bring fruit sweetness, while soy sauce adds savoriness. Loosen chilled rice, fry the aromatics, then toss until the whole pan is steaming. Whole-cut pork reaches 63°C / 145°F and rests for 3 minutes; ground pork requires 71°C / 160°F. This version has not been kitchen-tested, so its timing, yield, and texture still need human confirmation.
